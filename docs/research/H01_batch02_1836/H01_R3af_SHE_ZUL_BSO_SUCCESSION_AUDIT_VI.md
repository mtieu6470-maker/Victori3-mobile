## 1. Scope and method

R3af follows the R3ae snapshot audit by researching post-1836 officeholder succession for:

- **SHE — Shewa:** Sahle Selassie, Haile Malakot, Menelik II and the unresolved Bäzzabbǝh interval.
- **ZUL — Zulu polity:** Mpande, Cetshwayo and Dinuzulu, including exile/restoration and the transition to British colonial rule.
- **BSO — Basotho polity:** Moshoeshoe I, Letsie I and Lerotholi, distinguishing local paramount leadership from British/Cape colonial sovereignty.

The research target is **1836 through 1900 inclusive**. It tests identity, title, date precision, interruption of effective rule, succession legitimacy and the external authority actually exercising sovereignty. It does not assume that each state has one uninterrupted king list.

Sources include scholarly reference works (the *Encyclopaedia Aethiopica* and ODNB), peer-reviewed articles, South African History Online, a contemporary U.S. diplomatic report, and the contemporary Basutoland annual report. Inspection level and source limitations are recorded in `H01_R3af_SOURCE_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.csv`.

## 2. Executive findings

1. **Shewa cannot be modelled as a single uninterrupted succession from 1855.** Menelik was proclaimed after Haile Malakot's death but was captured and taken to Mäqdäla. He escaped and re-entered Shewa in 1865, displacing Bäzzabbǝh. Menelik's formal recognition as nəguś by Yohannes IV belongs to March 1878; his imperial claim/coronation belongs to 1889.
2. **Zulu kingship includes a 1879–1883 interruption and a colonial sovereignty break.** Cetshwayo became king after Mpande's death in 1872 (coronation in 1873), was exiled after the 1879 war, and was restored with reduced authority in early 1883. Dinuzulu succeeded/was proclaimed in 1884, but the British declared Zululand a possession in 1887; Natal's annexation followed in 1897.
3. **Basotho succession after Moshoeshoe is a layered authority sequence.** Letsie succeeded in 1870 at year precision. Basutoland became a British protectorate in 1868, was administered by the Cape Colony from 1871 and returned to direct Crown administration in 1884. The contemporary report records Letsie's death on 20 November 1891, chief support for Lerothodi and Crown recognition communicated in January/February 1892.

### 3.2 Interpretation

- The **1855 proclamation** and **effective governing authority** must be separate facts. The proclamation did not mean Menelik exercised uninterrupted government during his captivity.
- Bäzzabbǝh is mentioned as an independent ruler who was displaced in 1865, but no exact start/end dates or full territorial scope have been established. Do not create invented dates for him.
- Title progression is not a simple synonym list: Ras at Sahle Selassie's accession, self-proclaimed Negus c.1839 (R3ae), and the formal recognition of Menelik as nəguś in 1878 are different events.
- Menelik's imperial declaration in 1889 should trigger an entity/office transition decision. The research does not authorise an assumption that the same SHE head-of-state record should continue after the Ethiopian imperial office is established.
- **Source conflict:** Ege (2005) states Haile Malakot died 9 November 1855; Clapham (2007) says December 1855. Use 1855 only until underlying chronicle evidence is checked.

## 4. ZUL — Zulu polity

### 4.1 Candidate sequence

| Person / authority | Working interval | Office/title | Succession and status interpretation | Confidence |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Mpande kaSenzangakhona | 1840 – 19 Nov 1872 | King of the Zulu | Came to power after Dingane's defeat in 1840; Cetshwayo was his successor. Cetshwayo's growing control after 1856/1861 does not end Mpande's formal kingship. | Strong year and reported death day |
| Cetshwayo kaMpande — first kingship | 19 Nov 1872 – 1879 | King of the Zulu | Formal accession followed Mpande's death in 1872; official coronation took place in 1873. He was captured/exiled after the British invasion and defeat of 1879. | Strong accession year; day-level coronation and end need evidence |
| Colonial partition / Cetshwayo in exile | 1879 – early 1883 | British-imposed chiefdom arrangement; no single uncontested Zulu king exercising full territorial rule | Zululand was divided into chiefdoms under appointed chiefs. Cetshwayo remained the royal claimant/prisoner and was not exercising ordinary sovereign government. | Strong broad sequence; exact boundaries and legal terms require source-by-source handling |
| Cetshwayo — restricted restoration | Early 1883 – 1884 | Restored king over a reduced central area | SAHO says he was reinstalled early in 1883 but his powers were drastically reduced and a Resident monitored him. He died in 1884 according to ODNB and other SAHO records. | Broad sequence supported; exact death conflict remains open |
| Dinuzulu kaCetshwayo | 1884 – 1887 (as a local/royal claimant; not independent sovereign after annexation) | Proclaimed Zulu king by Boer allies / uSuthu leader | SAHO dates succession to 21 May 1884. The third-party recognition by Boer allies and the royal family's claim must not be confused with recognition by Britain or a restored independent state. | Year supported; original proclamation and recognition chain open |
| British colonial authority over Zululand | From proclamation 14 May 1887; Natal incorporation in 1897 | British possession/colony; Governor of Natal as Supreme Chief | The U.S. diplomatic report reproduces the notice of the 14 May 1887 proclamation. Cambridge's chapter says the Governor (also Governor of Natal) was to be Supreme Chief. In 1897 Zululand was incorporated into Natal. | Strong for the 1887 proclamation; 1897 transition supported by SAHO |
| Dinuzulu under colonial administration | 1889–1900 milestones | Prisoner/exile, then pardoned/returned and Government Induna (appointment date conflict) | SAHO reports conviction/exile in 1889, pardon/return in 1894 in one article and release/appointment as Government Induna in 1898 in another. He was not a sovereign head of an independent Zulu state after the 1887 annexation. | High for colonial subordination; 1894/1898 sequence needs clarification |

### 4.2 Interpretation

- Cetshwayo's 1856 victory secured his succession but did **not** make him king while Mpande remained alive; the ODNB extract explicitly places formal kingship in 1872.
- 1879–1883 is not a normal regnal interregnum: it is a war, exile and imposed territorial partition. Cetshwayo's restoration in 1883 was restricted to a central district and did not recreate the pre-war kingdom.
- Dinuzulu's succession in 1884 occurred in a civil-war context and relied on Boer assistance. The 1887 British proclamation changed the sovereign/legal status even though the royal family and Dinuzulu continued to matter locally.
- 1897 is a second, distinct colonial transition: the remaining Zululand territory was incorporated into Natal. Do not merge 1887 and 1897 into a single event.
- **Death-date conflict:** one SAHO narrative says Cetshwayo died in late 1883, but the ODNB heading and SAHO event/biography records identify 1884; an SAHO event gives 8 February 1884, while its own supporting detail is secondary. Use 1884 provisionally and keep the precise date open.
- **Dinuzulu status conflict:** SAHO's political-history article describes pardon/return in 1894 as a mere induna; the separate biography says he was released and appointed Government Induna in 1898. Do not collapse pardon, physical return and formal employment into one date.

## 5. BSO — Basotho polity / Basutoland

### 5.1 Candidate sequence

| Person / authority | Working interval | Office/title | Succession and status interpretation | Confidence |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Moshoeshoe I | 1836 – 11 Mar 1870 | Chief / Morena; later Paramount Chief in English-language sources | SAHO says he succeeded Mokhachane as chief of the Bamokoteli in 1820; he died 11 March 1870. | Strong for death date and 1820 year; exact accession day/title development open |
| Letsie I | 1870 – 20 Nov 1891 | Paramount Chief of the Basotho | Succeeded his father in 1870. No reliable contemporary evidence inspected establishes an exact accession day; do not copy day-level dates from modern king lists. | Strong for year and death day; start day open |
| Lerotholi / Lerothodi | Claim after 20 Nov 1891; Crown recognition 6 Jan 1892; public communication 5 Feb 1892 – 1905 | Paramount Chief | The contemporary report says Letsie died on 20 November; 24 principal chiefs petitioned for Lerothodi; a telegram of royal recognition arrived on 6 January and was communicated at a National Pitso on 5 February. The article by Maliehe says Letsie preferred Maama and the British confirmed Lerotholi, showing a competing succession narrative. | Strong on report chronology; customary legitimacy and preference dispute require more archival/oral-history work |

### 5.2 Colonial-status chronology (separate from the paramount chief list)

| Date | External political status | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 12 Mar 1868 | British protectorate | Moshoeshoe requested British protection while resisting the Orange Free State; the protectorate is not the same as full internal sovereignty |
| 1871 | Annexed to / administered through the Cape Colony | Cape administration affects the external sovereign authority while Moshoeshoe and later Letsie retained local leadership |
| 1880–1881 | Gun War against Cape colonial rule | Demonstrates that local paramount authority and colonial government were contested, not a single uncontested chain |
| 1884 | Direct Crown administration | Basutoland moved away from Cape Colony government to direct British administration; local paramount chiefs continued under colonial supervision |

### 5.3 Interpretation

- Keep **Moshoeshoe's chiefship**, his later supra-clan/paramount position and modern English translations such as “King” separate. The earlier R3ae audit already flagged that the exact Morena/Morena e Moholo chronology cannot be pinned down to a single date.
- Letsie's 1870 accession is safe at year precision; an exact start day is not.
- The colonial report provides a valuable contemporary sequence: death (20 Nov 1891), representation signed by 24 chiefs, Crown recognition (6 Jan 1892) and public communication at a National Pitso (5 Feb 1892). Its own author is a colonial official; it is not neutral proof of all customary claims.
- Maliehe (2021) reports that Letsie preferred Maama while the British confirmed Lerotholi. The contemporary report records chief support for Lerothodi. These may reflect competing customary, political and administrative aspects of succession; do not erase the conflict.
- From 1868 onward, a Basotho paramount chief should not be treated automatically as the sovereign head of an independent state. The modelling layer needs local officeholder and external colonial sovereign/controller as distinct roles.

## 6. Cross-entity modelling requirements for any future data patch

1. **Separate officeholder from effective controller.** This is essential for Menelik's captivity (1855–1865), Cetshwayo's exile/partition (1879–1883), and Basutoland's colonial administration (1868 onward).
2. **Represent interrupted/limited reigns explicitly.** Cetshwayo's pre-1879 reign and 1883 restoration should not be collapsed into one continuous interval without an interruption annotation.
3. **Separate a succession claim from its recognition.** Dinuzulu's royal/Boer proclamation, the British 1887 possession proclamation, and Lerotholi's customary support versus Crown recognition are different events.
4. **Preserve date precision and conflicts.** The source record supports years for several transitions, not exact days. If the current data schema requires ISO dates, do not invent day values; propose a schema-compatible uncertainty representation in a separately authorized task.
5. **Separate local polity leadership from state sovereignty.** “King”, “Paramount Chief”, “nəguś”, “Governor/Supreme Chief” are not interchangeable labels.
6. **Treat colonial annexation as a governance transition, not a personal royal succession.** ZUL 1887/1897 and BSO 1868/1871/1884 should be stored as external governance events with territorial scope.
