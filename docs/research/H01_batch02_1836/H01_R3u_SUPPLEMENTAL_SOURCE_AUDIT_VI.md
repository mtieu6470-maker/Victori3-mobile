## Decisions

- **OYO 650,000:** retain as `UNVERIFIED_SCOPE / QUARANTINED`. Law (1977), pp.295–302 remain access-restricted. The accessible table of contents confirms they are in Chapter 14, “The Fall of Oyo”, but the pages have not been read.
- **OYO_OYO 1,450,000:** retain as `UNVERIFIED_DERIVATION / QUARANTINED`. Manning (2014), Chapter 4, pp.131–150, provides continental and six macro-regional estimates; it has no Oyo/Yoruba-specific 1836 figure.
- **Oyo peak estimate:** Butcher & Griffiths (2021, p.385) say Oyo probably ruled more than 750,000 people at its peak c.1780, citing Asiwaju (1989, p.704). This is a peak-era secondary estimate, not a 1836 estimate. The English Asiwaju page itself remains unread. The same article describes Oyo as a decentralized suzerainty/tribute system with varying internal autonomy; this reinforces that a broad technical region join is not proof of direct control.
- **NDE 60,000:** reinterpret as a rough circa-1830s population estimate for the Mzilikazi-led polity’s subjects, not an ethnic Ndebele core. Lye (1969, pp.96–97) records 60,000–80,000 as an estimate; this is not a confidence interval. Lye separately reports 1,000–5,000 Nguni warriors in 1836.
- **NDE 45,000–85,000:** remains unsupported by the inspected Lye material; original Rasmussen (1978) derivation still needs inspection.
- **NDE_MOS 210,000:** broad geographic reconstruction only. Lye (1969, p.91) says raids beyond the Vaal and Limpopo did not amount to territorial control; the whole region cannot be treated as direct Ndebele subjects.
- **LIB_MON 3,500:** still `UNVERIFIED_MONROVIA_1836`. Hassell (1866, p.169) reports 3,500 US emigrants within a Liberia-wide total of c.5,000 in 1838, after listing multiple settlements. This is a possible source of confusion, not proven source lineage, and not a Monrovia-only 1836 census.
- **LIB_MON extra 100:** no independent source identified; remains unverified.
- **DZ_KRU 356,400:** arithmetic residual only (360,000 minus provisional LIB_MON 3,600), not a census or a single-polity population.

## Liberia source comparison

Shick (1971, pp.45–47) works from the 1844 Congressional Record’s individual emigrant register: 4,571 cumulative arrivals during 1820–1843, with 242 arrivals in 1836. At p.49 he reports 1,819 people still living in settlements in 1843 using his dataset. Starr (1913, pp.24–25) reproduces a “Population Movement for Liberia (exclusive of Maryland)” series whose “Pop.” column gives 2,230 for 1836 and 2,281 for 1838. These represent different time points and population universes and are not interchangeable with a Monrovia census. Freeman’s c.1837 estimate of about 1,500 Monrovia residents remains a separate near-contemporary city estimate.
